The short version

  • Your reports and the results extracted from them are stored on your device.
  • To read an uploaded or scanned report, Labwise sends its contents to Google’s Gemini AI service for parsing. Labwise asks for your permission before the first report is sent.
  • Manual entry does not use the AI service.
  • Labwise does not require an account, and does not include advertising or tracking.
  • You can delete all your data in the app at any time.

What Labwise handles

Labwise is an app for organizing and viewing laboratory results. Depending on how you use it, it handles:

  • Lab reports you upload (PDF or photo), scan with the camera, or enter by hand.
  • Results extracted from those reports, such as test names, values, units, reference ranges and dates.
  • Profile details you choose to enter, such as your name and sex, used to display the app and select reference ranges.
  • Settings, such as reminder interval and whether App Lock is on.

This information can include health information. It is yours, and Labwise handles it only to provide the features of the app.

Where your data is stored

Your reports, extracted results and settings are stored locally on your device. Labwise does not keep a copy on its own servers, and the developer cannot see your reports or results.

If you back up your device (for example with iCloud or a computer backup), app data may be included in that backup according to your device settings and Apple’s terms.

AI parsing with Google Gemini

To turn an uploaded or scanned report into structured results, Labwise sends the content of that report (its text and/or images) from your device to Google’s Gemini API. Google processes the content to return the extracted results to the app.

  • When: only when you choose to upload or scan a report, and only after you give permission.
  • What: the report you selected. Your other reports and results are not sent.
  • Why: to read the report and extract the results.
  • How it is handled by Google: Google processes this content under its own terms and privacy policy for the Gemini API. Please review them at ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms and policies.google.com/privacy.

Lab reports often contain personal details such as your name, date of birth and ID numbers. Only upload reports you are comfortable sending to this service. If you prefer not to, you can decline, and add your results with manual entry instead.

You can withdraw your permission at any time in Settings → Privacy & Data. Uploading and scanning will then be turned off. Existing data on your device stays until you delete it.

What Labwise does not do

  • It does not sell your data.
  • It does not use your data for advertising.
  • It does not include third-party analytics or advertising tools.
  • It does not require you to create an account.

Permissions the app may ask for

  • Camera, to scan a paper report.
  • Photos and Files, to choose a report you want to add.
  • Face ID / passcode, if you turn on App Lock. Labwise does not receive your biometric data; it is handled by iOS.
  • Notifications, if you turn on test reminders.

You can change these permissions at any time in your device’s Settings.

Your choices and controls

  • Withdraw consent for AI parsing in Settings → Privacy & Data.
  • Export your health data from Settings → Data Management.
  • Delete all data from Settings → Data Management. This permanently removes your reports and results from the app.
  • Delete the app to remove everything it stores on your device.

Data already processed by Google is governed by Google’s terms. For questions about your rights under the law where you live, email asnap48@gmail.com.

Medical disclaimer

Labwise is for information and organization only. It does not diagnose, treat or prevent any condition, and it is not a substitute for professional medical advice. Reference ranges can differ between laboratories and between people. AI-based reading can make mistakes, so always check extracted values against your original report and talk to a doctor about your results.

Children

Labwise is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them.

Changes to this policy

If we change how Labwise handles data, we will update this page and the date below. If a change is significant, the app will ask for your permission again.
